Theoretical Computer Science Theories

An overview of foundational theories in computer science.

What is Theoretical Computer Science?

Theoretical Computer Science (TCS) is a branch of computer science that deals with the abstract and mathematical aspects of computing. It encompasses various fields, including algorithms, computation models, complexity theory, automata theory, and formal languages. TCS provides the foundations for understanding what problems can be solved by computers, how efficiently they can be solved, and the limits of what can be computed.

History of Theoretical Computer Science

The roots of Theoretical Computer Science can be traced back to the early 20th century. Key milestones include:

  • 1930s: **Alan Turing** introduced the concept of the Turing Machine, laying the groundwork for computability.
  • 1936: **Alonzo Church** proposed the lambda calculus, which is a formal system for expressing computation.
  • 1960s: The development of formal languages and automata theory by researchers such as **Noam Chomsky**.
  • 1970s: The introduction of complexity theory and the classification of problems into complexity classes by **Stephen Cook** and others.
  • 1980s-1990s: The exploration of NP-completeness and advancements in algorithmic theory.
